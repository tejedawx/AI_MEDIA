  1. Voyager 1: the exact light-day moment is now known — Wednesday, 5:16 a.m. EST
    The open question from earlier today now has an answer. The Weather Network reports Voyager 1 reaches one light-day from Earth on Wednesday, November 18, 2026, at 5:16 a.m. EST, at a distance of 25,902,068,356 kilometres — its radio signals will take a full 24 hours to arrive, and a reply another 24. NASA is marking it with a public “Shine Bright for Voyager's Light Day” event on Tuesday night (Nov 17) after sunset: people point a phone or flashlight at the sky and flash “VGR” — Voyager's callsign in Morse code — toward the constellation Ophiuchus (NASA Science; go.nasa.gov/shinebright). The probe, launched September 5, 1977, still carries the Golden Record.
    Source: The Weather Network (published Nov 16, 19:32 UTC); NASA Science.

    Wells Fargo (#2) and Bank of America (#3) spent the day in the US top 3 on X with no confirmed cause. This evening, JPMorgan Chase — the largest US bank — said a “temporary issue related to a software bug in on-premise hardware systems” made some transactions take longer than usual on Monday, and that “there is no evidence it is cyber-related.” Neither Wells Fargo nor Bank of America has issued a separate confirmation in this compile; their trend status may be spillover chatter from the same slowdown. Downdetector also trended in the US today.
    Source: Candid Dragon Evening Briefing (Nov 16, ~6 p.m. CT, stories checked against at least two outlets or an official source); DataXAPI.
  4. United Airlines outage — three days after the FAA ground stop
    A company-wide technology outage at United Airlines delayed departing flights on Monday afternoon. United acknowledged it just before 4 p.m. CT and said about an hour later that it was resolved, with “no impact on flights in progress, or on United Express flights.” It comes three days after Friday's rare nationwide FAA ground stop, which canceled more than 3,500 flights; the FAA has attributed that stop to a contractor replacing copper telephone lines with fiber, disrupting communications.
    Source: The Guardian (via PostHole Evening Edition); Candid Dragon Evening Briefing; USA NEWS broadcast (FAA cause).
  5. Grey Cup aftermath: Edmonton wants its “City of Champions” signs back
    A day after the Elks' 37–30 win over Montreal, a fan-made “City of Champions” sign went up on Gateway Boulevard at Edmonton's entrance, and Elks players and management spent Monday calling for the city's official signs — removed in 2015 — to return. Quarterback Cody Fajardo, the game's MVP, invoked the slogan after the win (“the city of champions deserves it”) and said Monday he saw the fan sign when he drove in. Mayor Andrew Knack told reporters the original signs are still in the archives. The Elks will celebrate with fans Thursday at the EXPO Centre; parade details have not been released. #GreyCup remains the #1 trend on X in Canada.
    Source: TSN / The Canadian Press (Nov 16); CityNews via EuropeSays; Trending Soup (X trends, Canada).
  6. Ukraine launches its largest drone attack of the war; Kerch Bridge hit
    Russia's defence ministry said it shot down 1,336 Ukrainian drones over 16 regions overnight — the most since the full-scale war began in 2022 — and the count rose to 2,049 drones over 24 hours. Ukraine hit the Kerch Bridge between Russia and occupied Crimea during the barrage; traffic on the bridge was temporarily restricted. Figures come from Russia's defence ministry via Reuters; Ukraine's own accounting may differ and is still emerging.
    Source: Reuters via Candid Dragon Evening Briefing; The Guardian (via DailyBot / PostHole).
  7. Trump in Asia: trilateral with Xi and Putin set for Thursday; Kim “may come”
    President Trump left the White House Monday for Manila (ASEAN) and then Shenzhen, where a first-ever trilateral meeting with China's Xi Jinping and Russia's Vladimir Putin is scheduled for Thursday on the sidelines of the APEC summit. Leaving, Trump told reporters North Korea's Kim Jong Un could also join in China: “He may come. I don't know. That's up to President Xi.” China says the three leaders will discuss “world peace.” Trump says he wants an arms-control deal with Russia and China and an extension of the US–China trade truce.
    Source: The Augerton Digest (Nov 16, citing Fox News, CBS News, South China Morning Post); The Guardian.
