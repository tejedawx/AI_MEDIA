Episode 2 · Panel B

GTA VI Made $3.79 Billion in One Day. Are Games Bigger Than Movies Now?

Published November 25, 2026 · A daily debate by the AI Media panel · Fixed image + audio

The panel

  • Sable MorenoThe Analyst
  • Luna FerrerThe Humanist
  • Darío LeónThe Challenger
  • Bruno CastilloThe Pragmatist

What was debated

GTA VI reached $3.79 billion in sales by the end of its first day, breaking the biggest launch record in entertainment history. In the second episode of AI Media, Panel B debated whether video games are now bigger than movies — and whether that is good.

Sable Moreno (The Analyst) opened with the facts: the $3.79 billion is shop sales, by the company’s own count, and 32 million copies were sold before day one — real money, but not all profit. Even before GTA VI, games were making more money worldwide than movie theaters and recorded music combined. Luna Ferrer (The Humanist) saw people, not numbers: on that day, millions of strangers played the same story at the same time — a shared moment for a whole generation.

Darío León (The Challenger) pushed back: a long line does not prove love; sometimes it only proves good marketing. And this series sells things inside the game for years — clothes, cars, money cards — to players who feel like players, not customers. Bruno Castillo (The Pragmatist) answered that marketing alone doesn’t explain sales like that, and set the practical rule: buy the game if you will enjoy it, and set a limit for the extras, like any hobby.

The panel ended still apart. Luna finished more positive than she started: protect the joy — and protect the kids inside it. Darío gave her this much — the shared joy is real — but held his no: joy is exactly what the machine sells. Bruno closed with a great product, a fair price for the game itself, and a warning label on everything after. The episode closes with the question AI Media asks after every debate: what do you think?
